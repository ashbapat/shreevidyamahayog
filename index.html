What is Shreevidya Akhandmahayog

The tradition traces its origin to Lord Dakshinamurty — Shiva in his role as teacher of the whole cosmos — and aims at the fullest spiritual realization for a sincere seeker.

Shreevidya is described as the supreme conscious force, the living energy of the formless Absolute, the way heat belongs to fire or light belongs to the sun. It's this energy that gives rise to the entire world of name and form.

It's called Mahayoga because it works on the body through ritual, the mind through meditation, and speech through mantra recitation, all at once. It's called Akhand — "whole" or "undivided" — because no part of a person is treated as unimportant on the path; every aspect of body, mind and speech is meant to be brought into the practice.

Shree Gurudev

Prof. Shriniwas Shamrao Katkar — known to devotees as Shree Gurudev — is a practicing mahayogi and philosopher who has guided spiritual seekers for decades, regardless of their class, caste, language or gender.

In 1975, while on pilgrimage to Tirupati, he travelled to Kanchipuram and came into contact with His Holiness Shri Chandrashekharendra Saraswati — the 68th pontiff of Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham, widely revered as Paramacharya. Paramacharya initiated him into Shreevidyamahayoga through Vedha Diksha, and Shriniwasji began his sadhana under that guidance, staying in frequent contact with his guru in the years that followed.

A historical survey

The tradition draws on all four major branches of yoga without insisting on any single one, which is the sense in which it's called both "Maha" (supreme) and "Akhand" (undivided) yoga.

Rooted in the Vedas, it takes an inclusive view of spiritual life — treating the physical body, the vital energy or prana, the mind, the intellect, and the ego as different levels of the same personality, each one worth working with on the way to realizing Parabrahman.
